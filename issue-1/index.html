Cover story · ABM in action

30 ABM students took the DC Learning Journey

Washington, D.C. · Sept. 30–Oct. 3

Four days with the people who shape ag policy

Thirty ABM students spent September 30–October 3 in Washington, D.C., meeting the people who shape agricultural policy. Their conversations took them from congressional offices and USDA to meetings with Farm Bureau and industry advocates at the Capitol Hill Club. They met Senate and House Agriculture Committee staff, North Carolina congressional offices, and Senator Ted Budd.

One of ABM’s High-Impact Experiences, the trip was offered at no cost to students and organized by the Office of Student Mentoring board. Board member Justin Somers led the group, joined by board members Nelson Powell and Peter Thornton, with Madi Varron coordinating logistics. The next DC Learning Journey is planned for fall 2027.

“The DC Learning Journey didn’t just expose me to a career path in policy, it lit a fire in me to keep learning and push myself.”Mason Hoyle, ABM

Meet your people

A balcony pitch and a broken supply chain

Elena Wigglesworth · ABM ’28 · Roots Up Wellness

She pitched her business from a balcony and won

Afraid of heights? Elena Wigglesworth isn’t. At Entrepalooza’s Minute to Pitch It in September, she found out the stage was a balcony 12 feet above the judges. She went second and won $500 for Roots Up Wellness, her plan to bring yoga, fitness classes, and retreats to farms and green spaces. On October 22 she pitched it to Canadian billionaire Ronnen Harary, and she’s now in the running for the final round in December, with $25,000 at stake.

Minute to Pitch It is run by NC State Entrepreneurship, which helps any student turn an idea into a venture. Start with Kenneth Proseus, kpproseu@ncsu.edu.

“Start now!”Elena Wigglesworth, on starting a business as a student

Spooky Research Slam · Thu., Oct. 29, 4:30–6 p.m. · Fishbowl Forum, Hill Library

Brandon Estevez on where the avocado supply chain breaks

Brandon’s talk is “Ripe for Disaster: Where the Avocado Supply Chain Breaks.” His argument: cheap, always-in-stock avocados hide how fragile the system behind them is, from deforestation and cartel violence in Mexico to a handful of USDA inspectors at the border. He’s building a model to put a dollar figure on a disruption.

The project is self-directed through the Office of Undergraduate Research, with Dr. Kelsey Vourazeris as mentor, and it’s the first in a series he plans on supply chain fragility.

ABM in action

Three ABM students sat down with USDA

USDA roundtable · Patterson Hall · Oct. 21

This time Washington came to campus

On October 21, the USDA Office of Partnerships and Public Engagement visited NC State and asked for a student panel. Mason Hoyle, Brian Rodriguez, and Thomas Castillo spent an hour in Patterson Hall with USDA staff talking about student perspectives on agriculture, career pathways, and leadership, the issues facing young people in rural communities, and what they want to see from USDA next. Brian pressed them on support for land-grant universities and Extension, youth engagement, and federal relief for western North Carolina, including the new fuel relief program.

“Advocacy is a two-way street. It requires us to be willing to speak up for our communities when we go to DC, but also to build relationships where decision-makers are willing to listen when they come to NC State.”Brian Rodriguez, ABM

Game day is part of the weekly rhythm now

ABM intramurals

They lost by a lot and kept showing up

ABM sponsored three intramural teams this fall (flag football, indoor volleyball, basketball), rosters have more than doubled since the program started last spring, and classmates who knew each other only by face are lining up class schedules and trading internship tips between games. The volleyball team’s first game was against last year’s champions. They lost by a lot and kept showing up.

Spring adds 7-on-7 soccer as a full-season team, plus pickleball and kickball tournaments and trivia for anyone who can’t commit to a season. No experience, no fees.

Meet your people · Women in Agribusiness

Women in Ag started this fall, and it’s already three meetings in

ABM students built it from scratch: a kickoff where members set the group’s direction, an internship night where students explained how they landed theirs, and an election for the first officer team. It’s open to all women in agriculture. This spring they’ll host a roundtable with industry professionals on how to thrive in a male-dominated field.

Watch for the November meeting. Questions go to the new president, ABM senior Madison Martin
