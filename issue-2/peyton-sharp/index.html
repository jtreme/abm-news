Peyton Sharp: From ABM to the North Carolina Supreme Court

An ABM senior found the opportunity on LinkedIn, then reached out to every justice.
Peyton in the courtroom of the Supreme Court of North Carolina.

Peyton Sharp didn’t wait for an internship to find her. The ABM senior saw a LinkedIn post about opportunities at the Supreme Court of North Carolina, then reached out to every justice on the court to ask about openings in their chambers. Last spring, that initiative put her in Justice Trey Allen’s chambers at the state’s highest court.

Quotes lightly edited for length.
The ask

One LinkedIn post, seven justices

The application asked for a writing sample and a transcript, followed by an interview about her background, her interest in law, and why she wanted the job. But the step that mattered most came first: reaching out to the justices herself.

“One of my biggest takeaways from this experience is that you should never be afraid to reach out and ask about opportunities,” she said. “Sometimes taking the initiative and putting yourself out there can open doors you never expected.”

Inside the chambers

Petitions, memos, and oral arguments

Working alongside Justice Allen and his law clerks, Peyton drafted petitions for discretionary review, analyzed legal arguments, researched cases, and prepared summaries and memos for the chambers. She also sat in on oral arguments and watched how the justices worked through complex legal questions.

“I walked into the Supreme Court knowing I had so much to learn,” she said, “and I walked away with knowledge and experiences I never could have gained in a classroom.”

“You do not have to know everything or have the perfect resume to be deserving of an opportunity.”
Peyton Sharp, ABM ’27
An unexpected path

From agribusiness to law school

Peyton is from Clayton, minors in Agricultural Entrepreneurship, and serves as social chair of the ABM Club. She also works in leasing and marketing at The Station Raleigh. She knew she didn’t have the background people might expect for law. “My major is in agriculture, not political science or pre-law, but that didn’t mean I wasn’t capable of succeeding in a legal environment.”

She credits ABM with sharpening the skills the work demanded: critical thinking, analysis, and explaining her reasoning clearly. And she credits two sources of support. Her parents, Patty and Scooter Sharp, “have always encouraged me to pursue my goals, even when those goals seemed ambitious.” And Dr. Best: “Having a professor who believes in your potential and encourages you to pursue opportunities beyond the classroom makes a tremendous difference.”

The internship confirmed her plans. After she graduates in May 2027, Peyton wants to go to law school, with the University of Georgia as her top choice, and is especially interested in corporate and intellectual property law.

Her advice

Apply anyway

“One of the biggest mistakes students can make is counting themselves out of an opportunity before ever giving themselves a chance,” Peyton said. Her advice for getting ready: build relationships with professors, go to events, ask about opportunities even outside your major, and take writing seriously. “During my internship, I quickly realized how important it is to analyze information, communicate clearly, and pay attention to detail. Those are skills you can begin developing in almost any class or job.”

“Don’t wait until you feel completely prepared to start pursuing your goals,” she said. “Sometimes the best way to prepare yourself is to take that first step.”
