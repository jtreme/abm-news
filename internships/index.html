Programs and scholarships

NC State scholarships, leadership programs, study abroad, and research funding. Deadlines come from each program’s official page; check it before you apply.

Start here: scholarships
PACK ASSIST General Scholarship Application
One application that matches you to hundreds of NC State, CALS, and department scholarships. You have to reapply every year. CALS students also answer the CALS questions inside PACK ASSIST, and you should file the FAFSA too, since many awards consider need.
Who: Every undergraduate.
Opens Dec. 1. Priority deadline Feb. 15, 2027.
Go to PACK ASSIST ›

Scholarships for ABM students that PACK ASSIST matches you to include the ADAMA Scholarship (preference for ABM juniors and seniors), the NC Society of Farm Managers and Rural Appraisers Scholarship (first preference to ABM), the Randall V. and Erika W. Canady Scholarship (ARE students headed for agribusiness), and the Albert Gee Willcox Jr. Memorial Agricultural Scholarship. You don’t apply to these separately.

First-years only
Caldwell Fellows
NC State’s servant-leadership program, with training and a cohort of peers. All majors. Fellows currently receive $10,000 a year: $7,000 toward tuition and up to $3,000 for experiential learning.
Who: Full-time students in their first year at NC State with a 3.25 GPA or higher. You can apply only once.
Due Jan. 11, 2027, 5 p.m. Interviews Feb. 6; Selection Day Feb. 20.

Watch for these. Dates not posted yet.
  • McNair Scholars Program: prepares first-generation, income-eligible students with a 3.0 GPA or higher for a PhD, with summer research and graduate school support. Last year’s deadline was in March.
  • Paid summer research programs at NC State: the summer 2027 list posts in late fall. Deadlines usually run December through March.
  • Spring Undergraduate Research Symposium, Apr. 27–28, 2027: a good first place to present. The abstract deadline is usually late March.
  • Alpha Alpha Alpha (Tri-Alpha): the honor society for first-generation students with 30+ credits and a 3.3 GPA. Last year’s application ran mid-December to late January.
  • SECU Public Fellows: paid summer internships in public service across North Carolina. Last year’s deadline was in March.
  • CEFS Agroecology Scholars (ASPIRE): a paid summer research and extension program in sustainable agriculture. Last year’s deadline was at the end of January.
  • Alternative Service Break: week-long service trips during fall, winter, spring, or May breaks, on issues including agriculture, food insecurity, and conservation. Spring Break 2027 trips closed Sept. 30. May Break 2027 dates are expected soon, and travel awards help with trip costs.
  • AFA (Agriculture Future of America) Leaders Conference: a free leadership and career conference in Kansas City each November, attended by hundreds of ag students. Fifteen ABM students are going this year, Nov. 12–15. Applications are usually due in early September. AFA also offers scholarships through one application.
  • National fellowships such as Truman, Udall, and Fulbright: NC State nominates students, with campus deadlines months ahead of national ones. Talk to the University Fellowships Office early.

Clubs, teams, and ag organizations

Clubs on campus and professional associations with student memberships. Joining one is one of the easiest ways to meet people in the industry before you need a job.

Professional associations with student memberships
American Society of Farm Managers and Rural Appraisers (ASFMRA)
The professional group for farm managers, land appraisers, and ag consultants. Student members get mock interviews, resume reviews, and internship and job-shadowing leads through the Carolinas-Virginias chapter.
Who: College students in agriculture, business, economics, or real estate.
Cost: Free for students, through one year after graduation.
Join free ›
National Agri-Marketing Association (NAMA)
The national association for agricultural marketing and sales, with student marketing competitions, mentoring, and a national conference (Apr. 21–23, 2027). NC State is listed as a NAMA student chapter.
Who: College students interested in ag marketing and sales.
Cost: Ask the chapter.
Learn more ›
Agricultural and Applied Economics Association (AAEA)
The professional association for ag economists. The student section runs the Academic Bowl and other competitions at the annual meeting and connects you with economists and agribusiness professionals.
Who: Students, with a student membership.
Cost: $25 a year for students.
Learn more ›
North Carolina Cattlemen’s Association
The state’s beef industry group: producer connections, field days, scholarships, and an annual conference in February.
Who: Students interested in beef and livestock.
Cost: $10 a year for students.
Learn more ›
Soil and Water Conservation Society (SWCS)
Conservation careers and land stewardship, with an NC chapter, webinars, and career resources for students.
Who: Full-time students.
Cost: $40 a year for students.
Learn more ›
Carolina Farm Stewardship Association (CFSA)
Sustainable farming and local food systems in the Carolinas: discounted conferences, farm tours, workshops, and a job board.
Who: Anyone interested in food and farming. No farm needed.
Cost: Choose a “Friend” membership level.
Learn more ›
NC Grange
Local community and rural leadership groups, with committee and officer experience, public speaking, and service. Capitol Grange is in Wake County.
Who: Students can join; check the age track for youth events.
