Courses and order
Should I take ARE 301 before ARE 336?

Yes. Take ARE 301 first. Students who finish 301 before 336 come into 336 better prepared, with the concepts and math practice 301 gives them.

Does ARE 255 count for the AEE 226 requirement?

Yes. ARE 255 (Excel for Agribusiness) counts for the AEE 226 requirement, even though your degree audit may not show it yet.

Do Agricultural Institute (AGI) courses count toward my ABM degree?

No. AGI courses don’t count toward the ABM bachelor’s degree, not even as free electives. That includes ARE 104, 113, 115, and 132 and HS 121 and 141.

How many credit hours do I need to graduate?

120. Your degree audit shows which requirements you still need. ABM requirements in the catalog ›

Does ARE 433 count as one of my ARE 400-level electives?

No. ARE 433 is a required course for ABM, so it fills its own requirement, not one of your ARE 400-level electives.

Does ARE 492 (internship credit) count as an ARE 400-level elective?

No. ARE 492 doesn’t count toward your ARE 400-level electives. It counts in your Restricted Electives.

Math and statistics
What math does ABM require?

MA 121 is the only calculus ABM requires. You don’t need a two-course calculus sequence. After a few core economics courses, you can choose less math-heavy courses to finish the degree.

Why should I take MA 121 early?

MA 121 is the prerequisite for ARE 301, and ARE 301 is the prerequisite for ARE 433, a required course. If MA 121 waits, 301 and 433 wait too, and that can push back your graduation. Take MA 121 early.

Transfer credit
I want to take a class at a community college. How do I find the right one?

Start with the ABM transfer guide for NC community colleges. It lists the community college course that matches each NC State course, for example ECO 251 for EC 201, ECO 252 for EC 202, and MAT 263 for MA 121.

Community college course numbers are the same at every NC community college, so any school that offers that course number works. Look up the course number in the schedule at the community college you want, such as Wake Tech, or one near home for the summer.

For a course that isn’t in the guide, or a school outside North Carolina, check the NC State transfer database first, and confirm with your advisor before you enroll. Paw Plan’s scheduler can also show community college options for courses in your plan.

How will a course from a community college or another school transfer?

Look it up before you take it:

  • North Carolina schools, including community colleges: use the NC State Transfer Planner.
  • Any school: use the NC State transfer database. Choose the “Courses Taken at Other Institution” tab and search by school and course.

If the course isn’t listed, it has to be evaluated before you’ll know how it counts. NC State transfer credit ›

I took a class at a community college. How do I get it on my degree audit?

After your final grade posts, ask the other school to send your official transcript to NC State’s Office of Undergraduate Admissions through its electronic transcript service (such as Parchment), addressed to undergrad-admissions@ncsu.edu. A copy you email yourself won’t count. You need a C- or better. Courses already in the Transfer Credit Database post to your record automatically once the transcript arrives. Others have to be evaluated first. Then check your degree audit to see where the credit landed. Questions: transfercredit@ncsu.edu.

I think my transfer credit was evaluated wrong. What do I do?

First, check your degree audit to see how the credit applied, then talk with your advisor. If it’s still not resolved, submit a Transfer Credit Appeal Form. Details are on the transfer credit evaluation page.

Registering for classes
When is my registration time?

MyPack Portal › Student Homepage › Planning & Enrollment. Your enrollment date and time are listed there. Check Tasks & Holds on the Student Homepage too. A hold can block you from registering, so clear it before your time. Every ABM student has a Term Advisement Required hold on their account each semester. This is not a cause for concern.

How do I add classes to my cart?
  1. MyPack Portal › Student Homepage › Planning & Enrollment.
  2. In the left menu, choose Enrollment Wizard.
  3. Use the Add to Cart tab to search for classes and add them to your Shopping Cart.

You can fill your cart before your registration time opens. Check each class’s status (Open, Waitlist, or Closed). When your time opens, go to My Shopping Cart and enroll. Add a backup section or two in case your first choice fills. Stuck in the Enrollment Wizard? Call the Student Services Center at 919-515-NCSU (6278), weekdays 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Watch the video walkthrough ›   Enrollment Wizard help ›

My class is full. How do I get on the waitlist without losing my seat?

Use Swap to Waitlist. You keep your current section while you wait for the one you want:

  1. Add the section you want to your Shopping Cart.
  2. On My Shopping Cart, check the Wait List box for that section. Don’t click Enroll.
  3. Go to My Schedule, find the section you’re in now, and click Swap.
  4. Choose the section you want from the menu.

If a seat opens, you’re moved into the new section and dropped from the old one automatically. Don’t waitlist for more than one section of the same course. It causes enrollment problems.

Dropping and withdrawing
When is the drop deadline?

There are two dates to know, both on the academic calendar:

  • Census date (the 10th day of classes in fall and spring): drop before this and the class never appears on your transcript.
  • Drop/revision deadline (later in the semester): you can still leave a class yourself until this date, but it’s a withdrawal and shows a W.

After the drop/revision deadline, you need approval, and only for serious circumstances like a medical or family emergency. Dropping below full time can affect your financial aid and scholarships, so check before you drop. Talk with your advisor before you drop or withdraw.

What’s the difference between dropping a class and withdrawing?

Dropping a class before the census date means it never appears on your transcript.

Withdrawing from a class after the census date leaves a W on your transcript. A W doesn’t affect your GPA, but it counts as attempted hours, including for financial aid. You can withdraw from no more than 16 credit hours over your whole time at NC State, so use withdrawals carefully.

Grades
Can I take a class S/U (credit only)?

Only toward your free electives. You can count up to 12 credit-only hours toward graduation. An S means C- work or better, and neither an S nor a U counts in your GPA. Choose credit-only by the add/drop deadline on the academic calendar. Don’t take an ABM requirement S/U.

NC State grading rules ›

Can I get a bad grade removed from my GPA (grade exclusion)?

You can use up to two grade exclusions, on NC State courses where you earned a C- or lower. What to know first:

  • It’s permanent. Once an exclusion posts, you can’t remove it or move it to a different course.
  • The grade comes out of your GPA, but the course no longer counts toward your graduation requirements, so you’ll need another course to fill that requirement.
  • The course and grade still show on your transcript, marked as excluded, and the hours still count for financial aid progress.
  • Graduate and professional schools may add excluded grades back when they calculate your GPA.
  • Exclusions have to post before you apply to graduate.

Talk with your advisor before you use one. NC State grade exclusion rules ›

Can I retake a class?

Yes. You can repeat a course once without permission if you earned lower than a D+. Both grades count in your GPA unless you use a grade exclusion on the first one. Taking a course a third time takes your college dean’s permission.

NC State grading rules ›

What happens if I get an Incomplete?

An Incomplete (IN) gives you time to finish the work after the semester ends, and it’s up to you to finish it. If you don’t, the IN turns into an F at the university deadline. Make a plan with your instructor and stick to it.

Changing majors and adding a minor
What do I need to change my major to ABM (CODA)?

To apply, you need:

  • 12 or more graded NC State credit hours
  • A cumulative GPA of 2.0 or better
  • MA 121, MA 131, or MA 141 with a C- or better
  • ARE 201, EC 201, or EC 205 with a C- or better

Preference goes to students with:

  • A B- or better in ARE 304, ARE 321, or ARE 336
  • An NC State GPA of 3.0 or better

AP and transfer credit count the same as NC State courses.

Admission is competitive, and the minimums alone usually aren’t enough. The strongest applicants have taken the preferred courses and done well in them. Do the very best you can in every ARE class you take. Your ARE grades matter most. If you haven’t taken a preferred course yet, take one, then apply.

How do I declare a minor?

Add it yourself in MyPack:

  1. Log in to MyPack Portal and open the Student Homepage.
  2. Choose Planning & Enrollment.
  3. In the left menu, choose CODA: Manage Majors/Minors, then Add a Minor.

Most minors require at least 12 NC State credit hours and a 2.0 GPA, and some require certain courses first. Check the minor’s requirements before you apply. A popular choice for ABM students is the Agricultural Entrepreneurship minor.

Internships for credit
How do I get credit for my internship (ARE 492)?

Get your ARE 492 contract signed before your internship starts. Your employer will be asked to verify the internship, and your answers on the contract need to be specific to you and your internship.
