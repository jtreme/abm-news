Luke Morgan: Farm Management, from the other side of the room

An ABM junior went back to a class he enjoyed, and now talks about it in job interviews.
Luke Morgan
ABM junior · Teaching assistant, ARE 303

Luke Morgan is an ABM junior from Currituck and an Undergraduate Teaching Assistant (UTA) for ARE 303, Farm Management. He applied after a conversation with Dr. Treme about getting involved in ABM, and he chose ARE 303 because he enjoyed the class and liked the professor, Dr. Best.

Quotes lightly edited for length.
The job

Every class, every week

Luke attends each class, helps run classroom activities, grades in-class assignments, and reviews course concepts with students.

The moment

The last constraint

During an in-class linear programming assignment, a student had graphed two constraints but was stuck on the third, which looked different from the first two. Luke showed the student how to set it up the same way as the others, and the next steps fell into place. “It showed me how simply rephrasing a problem can help a student succeed,” he said.

“As long as you performed well in the class, you are more than qualified.”
Luke Morgan, ABM junior
What surprised him

His own experience helps, and he keeps learning

Two things surprised Luke: how much his own time as a student in the class helps him connect with students now, and how much more he learns working through the course a second time.

What he’s gained

Leadership, organization, and mentors

Luke says he has become a better leader, a better task manager, and more organized. He’s also gained the confidence to answer questions about course material on the spot and built relationships with meaningful mentors.

In interviews

“Leadership experience within a job setting”

Being a UTA has come up in several of Luke’s interviews. He describes it as leadership experience in a job setting: helping students in the right direction while helping Dr. Best with whatever the course needs.

His advice

You can build the rest on the job

“Communication, collaboration, and leadership are all skills you can develop throughout your experience in the role,” Luke said. His one-sentence version: “Being a UTA allows you to apply what you have previously learned, create meaningful connections with department faculty, and make new friends, all while getting paid.”
