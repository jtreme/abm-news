Want to do research? How to ask a professor to mentor you

Dr. Kelsey Vourazeris, Assistant Professor, Department of Agricultural and Resource Economics.

Dr. Vourazeris is in her first semester in ARE and has hit the ground running in the undergraduate program, including mentoring Brandon Estevez's research on the avocado supply chain. We asked her what students should know before asking a professor for help with a project.

Answers lightly edited for length.
Question 1

What's your research about, in a sentence a sophomore would understand?

I study how technology, trade, and business decisions affect the food we grow, what it costs, and how it gets to your plate. Alongside that work, Brandon and I are studying whether cartel violence in Mexico affects the avocado prices U.S. consumers pay.

Question 2

Brandon asked you to mentor his avocado project. What made you say yes?

Brandon brought real curiosity about what happens behind the scenes in the avocado industry. His project connects agriculture, trade, and crime, and I liked that he wanted to explore a complicated issue.

"Students don't need to have everything figured out for me to be interested in helping them."
Dr. Kelsey Vourazeris
Question 3

What should a student have ready before asking a professor to mentor research?

Have a topic you're curious about, a rough question you'd like to explore, and a sense of why it interests you. You don't need a polished proposal. It also helps to think about how much time you can commit and what you hope to learn.

Question 4

What's the most common mistake students make when they approach faculty?

Waiting until they think their idea is perfect. Part of mentoring is helping you turn a broad interest into a question you can actually answer. Come prepared, but leave room for the idea to develop.

Question 5

What are you teaching this spring?

ARE 311, Agricultural Markets. Students explore what drives food and agricultural prices and how producers and businesses navigate changing markets.

Question 6

One thing about you students wouldn't guess?

I really enjoy trivia. I like learning a little about a lot of different things, and it's always fun when a random fact I've picked up turns out to be useful on trivia night.
