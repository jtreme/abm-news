Ashling Murphy: From ABM to the grain desk

An ABM alum on merchandising corn for a poultry company, grad school at Kansas State, and saying yes.
‹ Back to Issue 3
Ashling Murphy
ABM alum · Senior Grain Merchandising Manager, Mountaire Farms

Ashling Murphy is an ABM alum and Senior Grain Merchandising Manager at Mountaire Farms, a poultry company with operations in North Carolina and Delaware. She sent us Mountaire’s two summer grain internships, which are open now.

Answers lightly edited for length.
The job

Rail corn for six feed mills

Ashling merchandises rail corn for Mountaire’s six feed mills and helps oversee farmer truck merchandising and grain logistics across truck, rail, and containers.

In a typical week, she monitors feed mill capacity, usage, and forward coverage, reads commodity market news, and works with the sales and transfer teams on the week’s sales commitments and grain movements.

Why ABM

Agriculture, and people

“I chose ABM because I knew I wanted to work in agriculture, but I also knew I wanted a career where I could work with people,” she said. In high school she was on the science side, through crop science, but she grew more interested in the business and economics of agriculture.

She still uses what she learned in microeconomics and commodity markets when she reads market news and works out what it means for Mountaire’s merchandising strategy.

Grad school

A master’s at Kansas State

Ashling earned a master’s in agricultural economics at Kansas State University, on the recommendation of Dr. Treme and Dr. Piggott. It was a good fit: she wrote her thesis on agricultural policy, her favorite class as an undergraduate. Kansas also gave her the chance to learn about water economics and livestock marketing.

The path

From farmer marketing to the grain desk

After grad school, she joined Mountaire as an Agribusiness Specialist in farmer marketing. She supported the farmer merchandising team with marketing materials and helped launch an app that lets farmers sell grain and see their tickets, contracts, and settlements. Next she took on transfer planning, moving grain from Mountaire’s elevators to its feed mills and export locations. Today she is Senior Grain Merchandising Manager.

“Be willing to say yes to opportunities, even when they aren’t necessarily part of the path you originally pictured.”
Ashling Murphy, ABM alum
What stands out

Drive, attitude, and wanting to learn

When she looks at students, Ashling notices the ones who are driven, have a good attitude, and are eager to learn.

Her advice

Say yes

Transfer planning wasn’t a job Ashling pictured for herself. “It gave me a much better understanding of logistics, merchandising, and operations that I still use today,” she said.
