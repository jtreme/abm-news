ABM in action

Three ABM students pitched ideas for the coast at the Blue Economy Innovation Program

A free, two-day workshop on innovations for what’s caught and grown in coastal North Carolina.
From left: Wade McCallum, Lindsey Hockstra, Dr. Amit Sawant, and Meredith Newton.

The Blue Economy Innovation Program brings students, entrepreneurs, industry professionals, and educators together to develop solutions to challenges facing coastal communities and the broader blue economy. This fall, ABM students Lindsey Hockstra, Wade McCallum, and Meredith Newton went, along with ABM professor Amit Sawant.

What they did

Two days of panels, a team challenge, and a pitch

The sixth annual workshop, hosted by NC State in Morehead City the weekend of Oct. 24, brought together students, researchers, and professionals from NC State, UNC Wilmington, and Carteret Community College. This year's theme was "Innovations for What's Caught and Grown in Coastal NC." It opened with Karen Amspacher of the Core Sound Waterfowl Museum and Heritage Center, who spoke about how gentrification, globalization, sea level rise, and fishing regulations threaten Down East traditions. Day one's panels covered challenges and opportunities in the blue economy, including food innovation, technology, and aquaculture. Day two turned to building a venture: entrepreneurship development, the legal side of structuring a business, and funding for student entrepreneurs, plus students sharing their own innovations. The group also explored the Down East community and toured Carteret Community College's aquaculture facility.

Then participants broke into teams led by researchers and industry experts. Each table took on one problem, developed a sustainable solution, and pitched it.

Wade's team took on marketing for the North Carolina oyster industry, which faces logistics problems, marketing hurdles, and a crowded market. Their pitch: a marketing cooperative for NC oysters, with targeted campaigns, myth-busting about eating oysters, agritourism, distinctive seafood labeling, and member pricing. They even wrote a slogan: "Get Local, Stay Salty, and Shuck Sustainably!"

The workshop was free. Lodging was covered, breakfast and lunch were provided, and free transportation was offered.

The ABM connection

Part of ABM's agricultural entrepreneurship courses

The program lines up with ABM's agricultural entrepreneurship series, ARE 370 and ARE 470. Lindsey and Wade heard about it when program director Dr. Christopher Osburn visited their ARE 470 class with Dr. Amit Sawant, and many of the topics matched the project their class is working on now. Meredith heard about it in ARE 370.

Lindsey at Carteret Community College's aquaculture facility.
In their words

Why they went

Lindsey: "I wanted to build my entrepreneurial skills and learn more about the challenges our coastal communities face. I was excited to participate in a program where I could combine my entrepreneurial experience with creative thinking to explore solutions to real-world problems."

Wade: "I attended because I wanted to expand on my entrepreneurial skills and learn from a variety of perspectives on ocean innovation and sustainability."

"I was excited to bridge the gap between two passions, coastal communities and entrepreneurship."
Lindsey Hockstra, ABM

What they got out of it

Wade, who grew up 30 minutes from the Down East communities the workshop focused on: "Having grown up just 30 minutes away, I've seen firsthand how these challenges affect my friends and their families." He also took away that sustainability has three dimensions, social equity, economic viability, and environmental conservation, and that weighing all three makes a stronger pitch.

Wade: "I learned about the pressures that coastal communities are facing and how to create solutions that are rooted in sustainability and innovation. I also learned more about my hometown's blue economy and how I can get involved to give back to it. I connected with industry experts to help me with my research on aquaculture farms, and entrepreneurs to guide me on post-graduation opportunities and venture development."

Lindsey: "I would highly recommend this workshop to anyone interested in learning more about the challenges facing our coastal communities, developing their entrepreneurial skills, exploring the blue economy, and networking with industry professionals."

How to get involved

Students who want to go deeper can take his fall courses for internship or research credit, including MEA 498 or 499 for undergraduates and MEA 611 or 791 for graduate students. Interested in entrepreneurship more broadly? Look at ARE 370 and ARE 470.
