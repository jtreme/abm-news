Meet your people
Elena Wigglesworth pitched her business from a balcony and won
Elena with the Minute to Pitch It check, Talley Student Union, September 17.
Afraid of heights? Elena Wigglesworth isn't. At Entrepalooza's Minute to Pitch It in September, she found out the stage was a balcony 12 feet above the judges. She went second and won $500 for Roots Up Wellness, her plan to bring yoga, fitness classes, and retreats to farms and green spaces. On October 22 she pitched it to Canadian billionaire Ronnen Harary, and she's now in the running for the final round in December, with $25,000 at stake. Minute to Pitch It is run by NC State Innovation and Entrepreneurship, which helps any student turn an idea into a venture; start with Kenneth Proseus, kpproseu@ncsu.edu. Matt Smith has the full story.
“Start now!”
Elena Wigglesworth, ABM ’28, on starting a business as a student

Meet your people
Brandon Estevez is presenting at the Spooky Research Slam
Thursday, Oct. 29, 4:30–6 p.m., Fishbowl Forum, Hill Library. Brandon's talk is "Ripe for Disaster: Where the Avocado Supply Chain Breaks." His argument: cheap, always-in-stock avocados hide how fragile the system behind them is, from deforestation and cartel violence in Mexico to a handful of USDA inspectors at the border. He's building a model to put a dollar figure on a disruption. The project is self-directed through the Office of Undergraduate Research, with Dr. Kelsey Vourazeris as mentor, and it's the first in a series he plans on supply chain fragility.
“If you eat an avocado here in North Carolina, it was likely grown by farmers 2,000 miles away you'll never meet.”
Brandon Estevez, ABM

ABM in action
30 ABM students took the DC Learning Journey
ABM students on the Capitol steps, Washington, D.C., October 2026.
Thirty ABM students spent September 30–October 3 in Washington, D.C., meeting the people who shape agricultural policy. Their conversations took them from congressional offices and USDA to meetings with Farm Bureau and industry advocates at the Capitol Hill Club. They met Senate and House Agriculture Committee staff, North Carolina congressional offices, and Senator Ted Budd.
One of ABM's High-Impact Experiences, the trip was offered at no cost to students and organized by the Office of Student Mentoring board. Board member Justin Somers led the group, with Madi Varron coordinating logistics. The next DC Learning Journey is planned for fall 2027.
“The DC Learning Journey didn’t just expose me to a career path in policy, it lit a fire in me to keep learning and push myself.”
Mason Hoyle, ABM
 
ABM in action
Three ABM students sat down with USDA
Mason Hoyle and Brian Rodriguez outside Patterson Hall after the USDA roundtable, October 21.
This time Washington came to campus. On October 21, the USDA Office of Partnerships and Public Engagement visited NC State and asked for a student panel. Mason Hoyle, Brian Rodriguez, and Thomas Castillo spent an hour in Patterson Hall with USDA staff talking about student perspectives on agriculture, career pathways, and leadership, the issues facing young people in rural communities, and what they want to see from USDA next. Brian pressed them on support for land-grant universities and Extension, youth engagement, and federal relief for western North Carolina, including the new fuel relief program.
“Advocacy is a two-way street. It requires us to be willing to speak up for our communities when we go to DC, but also to build relationships where decision-makers are willing to listen when they come to NC State.”
Brian Rodriguez, ABM
 
ABM in action
15 ABM students are headed to Kansas City
The AFA Leaders Conference (Nov. 12–15) is a leadership and career conference for students headed into food and agriculture. NC State is sending 61 students this year, its largest group ever. Fifteen are ABM students: Braden Armstrong, Grace Bailey, Elizabeth Bennett, Daniel Davalos-Martinez, Cynthia DeVine, Nithin Gudderra, Andrew Henderson, Nia Long, Paige Meadows, Shannon Purnama, Jacob Rase, Brian Rodriguez, Dylan Rueda Ibanez, Preston Sharp, and Brett Wimberly. Nithin Gudderra is on AFA's Student Advisory Team and recruited many of the attendees.
“I was born in Kansas City, and AFA and NC State have come together to let me go back, not as a tourist, but as an upcoming business professional.”
Grace Bailey, ABM ’27, ABM Ambassador

ABM in action
ABM intramurals
The ABM volleyball team, fall 2026. Back: Alec, Grace, Jacob. Front: Delaney, Alexis, Connor.
Game day is part of the weekly rhythm now. ABM sponsored three intramural teams this fall (flag football, indoor volleyball, basketball), rosters have more than doubled since the program started last spring, and classmates who knew each other only by face are lining up class schedules and trading internship tips between games. The volleyball team's first game was against last year's champions. They lost by a lot and kept showing up. Spring adds 7-on-7 soccer as a full-season team, plus pickleball and kickball tournaments and trivia for anyone who can't commit to a season. No experience, no fees.
“I wanted to get involved and meet new people while doing something I liked. What I got out of it: being able to work together with strangers will take you a lot further than just being good at something on your own.”
Connor Jackson, ABM freshman
“I joined volleyball because I never played sports. I wanted a fun way to connect with others while disconnecting from the pressure of academics. Every time someone makes a good shot, the other teammates vocalize encouragement. It really brought us together.”
Stephanie Merritt, ABM junior and transfer student

Women in Ag started this fall, and it's already three meetings in
ABM students built it from scratch: a kickoff where members set the group's direction, an internship night where students explained how they landed theirs, and an election for the first officer team. It's open to all women in agriculture. This spring they'll host a roundtable with industry professionals on how to thrive in a male-dominated field. Watch for the November meeting. Questions go to the new president, ABM senior Madison Martin, mlmarti9@ncsu.edu.
